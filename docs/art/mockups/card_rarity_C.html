Card rarity C: the store's own promo language

Your note on V3: "I would love if cards embrace rarity instead of just having a halo." Direction C uses the supermarket's own signage, so the card itself changes. A common card is a plain shelf label. A rare card is a SPECIAL OFFER shelf talker with a striped border, a starburst behind the product and a tilted RARE! price-gun flash. A legendary card is the jackpot display: a brass marquee frame with running bulbs (the machine's own sign), the product on a lit pedestal, a prize rosette with a ribbon banner, and confetti when it lands. V3's size (72×122 art px), name band, rule, B2 corner chips, A2 digits and S1 gold sticker don't change. The halo is gone: its chase lights moved into the legendary card's frame. 1:1 in the toolbar shows real 1080p pixels, and Reduced motion shows the still versions.

1 · One product at each rarity, in colour and in grey

Milk at common, rare and legendary, then the same three in greyscale. You can tell the three apart by shape alone: a flat tab and a thin edge, a spiky flash and a striped border, a rosette and a dotted bulb frame. Each card replays its landing every 5 s.

2 · Every kind at its rarity

Crisps and the Breakfast sticker (common), 2 for 1 and Mixed basket with its LAST ROW / BEST ROW sticker (rare), Shelf upgrade and Bakery aisle run with a WASTED row (legendary).

3 · Rarity is not the GOLDEN tier

The GOLDEN tier recolours the sprite (milk_golden). Legendary never touches the sprite or the art window's kind colour: its gold is all brass and bulbs around the paper. Left: a common Golden milk (gold product on a plain card). Middle: a legendary Milk (plain product on a marquee card). Right: both together.

4 · In the machine

The live loop replays the whole visit with the cards landing. The toolbar switches between Classic and Premium and can freeze a state.
