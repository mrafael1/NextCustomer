1What do you choose between?Q1

Three ways to turn the one random cart into a choice of three. The board deals each one at the same pace and counts the cards you must read to compare before shift 1. The 20-product fresh stock is small, so three random carts look alike, and A and B ask a new player to compare 39 cards.

  • A · Three random carts. Run today's dealer three times; each cart is fair-dealt. No new data. The carts share 4 staples and draw 8 of the same 20 products, so they differ by a card or two: a choice that barely matters, and the most to read (39 cards).
  • B · Three family-themed carts. The dealer leans each cart toward one family (its family coupon, then products drawn 2 : 1 toward the theme). Clear plans with a name and a colour. Still 39 cards; new weighted draw logic; the Produce, Bakery and Snack themes are thin until capsule products join the draw (#90).
  • C · Shared cart + pick 1 of 3 starter packs. Your 4 staples and 6 products from today's stock are the same whichever you pick; you choose one pack of 3 cards that work together. Still 13 cards. 9 cards to compare, each pack a named plan; it reuses the machine's "keep one of three" language; the reveal keeps its shape (the 9-slot picks row becomes 3 from the pack + 6 from the stock). Six packs to author and balance (section 4); the offer and the pick go in the save.
Recommended: C. It is the only one that is both a real choice and lighter than today's reveal. Default: no reroll button (the offer is saved, so quitting doesn't reroll either).

Recommended: A. Pictures are the dispenser's existing look, they cost no reading, and they give the pick something to react to. On the first run there is no choice anyway (Q3 A).

4The starter packsDEFAULT

Six packs, three offered per run. Each is three cards from the fresh stock (no capsule products) with one plan of its own, so no two packs in an offer overlap. Every pack holds exactly one Rare and two Commons (the machine's rarities, data/balance/machine.tres) and no Legendary, so the machine keeps its Legendary reveal. The pack's colour is the family its cards boost, as on the tickets. No pack depends on a card it doesn't hold, and none holds Soup (its "pays nothing beside Frozen" is a trap for a first plan). These are a first draft; the card picks are yours to change.

Price is not the measure. A coupon's price is €0, so the packs' price totals run from €1 (Coupon clipper, 2 coupons) to €10 (Deli counter, no coupon). The balance pass compares the packs by the sensible player's shift-1 totals and run win rates per pack, not by price. Stamps wait for #108. The first draft said which loyalty stamp each pack "fits at start". #108 is reworking the stamps (Coupon specialist was already cut down), so the packs are designed on scoring alone and the last column is only for #108: if it keeps build stamps, the offer rule becomes "never two packs that fit the same stamp" (Deli counter and Big shop would both only lean to Mixed basket, which about 97 % of today's carts fit anyway).

A first-time player already found the start overwhelming. A · Run 1 the given pack shows as a pack face: "your starter pack, choose one from your next run"

B · Every run "GOOD FIRST PICK" on Breakfast run, raised for you, until your first win

C · Every run three equal packs, nothing marked

  • A · Packed for you on run 1. Run 1 always gets Breakfast run (it never varies: it works with the Milk and Eggs already in the cart and has the plainest plan). It drops as a pack face labelled YOUR STARTER PACK with "choose one from your next run", then its cards fly into TODAY'S PICKS, so the idea of a pack is learned before it is a choice. The choice appears from run 2. The "first run" flag is a once-per-profile ProfileState.seen entry, the mechanism #67 uses for its run intro, set when run 1's reveal ends. The first run is no heavier than today. Freedom waits one run, and playtest friends usually play one or two runs, so the choice itself gets little playtest data (the playtest build could preset the flag; not planned).
  • B · Always choose, one marked. The three packs every time; Breakfast run carries a "GOOD FIRST PICK" ribbon and is raised for you (one confirm to accept) until the first win. Choice from the first second, and every playtest run logs a pick. Still a decision the new player can't judge; the ribbon may read as "the right answer" later.
  • C · Always choose, none marked. The full weight on the new player.
Recommended: A. It is the Basket Rush rule again: the first run teaches, the second run chooses. The pack face on run 1 teaches the word "pack" for free.

6May the Banana pack go past the 2-copy limit?Q4

The dealer never puts a 3rd copy of a card in the cart (copy_max 2), and your staples already hold 2 Bananas. A pack is hand-made, so it could be the one exception: 4 Bananas make the longest chain at the start. The rows below follow the #106 rules on PR #109: a Banana right after a Banana adds that Banana's price; anything between them starts a new chain; a multiplier on a middle link pays once and never carries.

The totals are traced by hand from the rule (core/rules/adjacent_match_bonus_rule.gd) and its test rows in tests/core/banana_chain_test.gd (e.g. banana, two_for_one, banana, banana pays 2, 0, 8, 6: "2 for 1 links the first two (2 + 2) × 2 = 8; the next adds 4, not 8"); the engine has not computed these exact rows. The first draft's "€20 before 2 for 1 doubles one" was the 4 plain links; with 2 for 1 before the last Banana the row is €28.

  • B · No: the Banana split. 2 for 1 + 2 Yogurt. It plays the 2 Bananas you already have: 2 for 1 links them, and a Yogurt pays +3 right after a Banana. No rule exception; a Banana plan that is one plan among six, not the staples' own mechanic turned up. No pack reaches Bulk buyer at the start (if #108 keeps that stamp).
  • A · Yes: the Banana bunch. 2 for 1 + 2 Bananas: 4 Bananas in the cart, a chain of 2, 4, 6, then 2 for 1 doubles the 4th: €28 in 5 slots. The loudest plan, and a 4-long chain no cart can reach at the start today.
Risks of A (why it is not the recommendation)
  • It brings back the repetition you reported on 2026-10-22. 4 Bananas, 2 for 1, Milk and Eggs are 7 cards: exactly the 7 general slots. A hand of 8 from 13 rarely holds all 7 (about 1 hand in 200 before redraws), but every hand pulls toward the same row, and the shared 6 barely matter to it.
  • It reads as the right answer. Its plan is the staples' own mechanic, so it looks like the intended pick, and it is strong: Eggs ×2 on the first Banana seeds the chain at 4, so Eggs, 4 Bananas, 2 for 1 and Milk trace to about €44 (1 + 4 + 6 + 8 + 0 + 20 + 5) against shift 1's €29.
  • It reaches the jackpot early. The Golden Banana row doubling (4, 12, 28, 60, kept on purpose as a rare jackpot) needs many Bananas; this pack starts with 4.
  • Code exceptions. The dealer's copy_max and the run save's could_deal (both in core/starting_deck.gd) and the simulator's dealing all need a pack exception.
Recommended: B. Take A only if the final balance pass shows the Banana bunch isn't the pick that beats the other packs.

7Defaults (stated, not asked)DEFAULTS

The offer

Any 3 of the 6 packs (20 possible offers), drawn first on the run seed's starting_deck stream, so the same seed and profile give the same offer. A pack's cards come with the pack, like the staples, whatever today's stock holds. The deck stays 13/15: 4 staples + 3 from the pack + 6 shared. The shared 6 are products only, so the cart's coupons come from the pack (Deli counter's cart has none, Coupon clipper's has 2).

Today's 6: one deal for all three

The shared 6 are dealt once, after the offer and before the choice, and are the same whichever pack you take (Q2 only decides whether you see them). The 2-copy limit counts the staples plus the most copies any offered pack holds: no Banana while the Banana bunch is offered, at most 1 more Dented can beside Bargain bin, no Yogurt beside the Banana split.

Fair deal, with a fallback

Each of the three carts (staples + pack + shared 6) gets today's check (8 sampled hands, 1 must reach the quota).
  • A pack whose cart fails is swapped for an unoffered pack that passes with the same shared 6, so a weak pack never steers the shared 6 for the others.
  • Only if no unoffered pack passes are the shared 6 dealt again (at most fair_deal_redeals, 4) and the check starts over.
  • After that, the deal with the most passing carts (then hands) is kept, as today's dealer does. It always ends.
Three carts cost about 30 ms in a debug build. Since v0.56's 7 general slots every first deal passed on a new profile (200 seeds), so swaps should be rare: to re-check with packs in the balance pass.

Raise, then confirm

The pick lasts the whole run, so it takes two steps, as the machine's CONFIRM on the push bar (2026-10-23): a click raises a pack (another click changes it), the push bar or Enter takes it; 1–3 raise. The reveal's skip (TAP_KEYS in ui/cart_reveal/cart_reveal.gd: any click, Space, Enter, Esc) changes during this step:
  • during the deal, a skip jumps to the choice, never to the summary;
  • during the choice, a click off the packs, Space and Esc do nothing;
  • only a confirmed pack ends the step; after it, a tap skips to the summary as today.

Saved before you choose

The run, its offer and today's 6 are saved before the choice opens, in the next run save format (PR #109 is format 5 and feature/readability may bump it again, so the number is set when it merges): the three pack ids, the shared 6 and, once taken, the pick. Continue shows the same offer, and quitting can't reroll. No reroll button.

Light faces, tickets on hover

A pack face shows its name, family colour and glyph, 3 pictures (a coupon on a coupon slip) and one line. Hovering or raising a pack opens it in place to its three tickets (#105 style), so nothing covers the staples. The tickets also show in the Cart afterwards. Reduced motion: the packs appear in place, no drop.

Telemetry and the simulator

A start_choice event after run_start (the offered pack ids, the shared 6, the pick, decide_ms, swaps and re-deals); the checkout timer still starts after the reveal. The simulator gets a pick policy per strategy (greedy: best shift-1 total; random; build:<id>: the pack whose plan the build needs) and per-pack win rates, measured only in the final balance pass.

What it is not

Not an unlock system: all six packs from the first choice on. Not a deck builder: Basket Rush (§7.3, after launch) builds the whole starting deck and is skipped on the first run; "picks (0–3)" may come later with #65; family decks with their own staples stay #90. The plan's §7.2 and the changelog get a line when it ships.

Names

"Starter pack" in the UI (not "deck": the cart is the deck), pack names as on this page. The reveal's line becomes "4 staples + 3 from your pack + 6 from today's stock".
