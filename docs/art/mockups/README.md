## What is real and what is not

- **Real:** the item sprites, icons and scenery in `assets/art/` (copied from `feature/items-and-scanner`, since `main` doesn't have them yet), and the three fonts, loaded from the repo's `fonts/`. Item rules, payouts and fizzle texts are the ones from the #79 screenshot.
- **Approximated:** every frame (belt, counter, labels, basket, monitor, register, buttons) is flat CSS in the palette, not the 9-slice pixel-art frames. B2's counter, belt and ledge are SVG polygons from one vanishing point (slat ends rounded to 2 px), which a pixel artist can repeat: in the game the belt would be a tiled texture in a trapezoid mask with a 9-slice frame, and the stanchions are atlas slices of `rope_post` and `velvet_rope`.
- **Placeholders:** the customer silhouettes in B and B2. Real customer art would be new scope.

### Re-generating

Each page has a generator in `planning_effects_src/gen_<name>/` (`a`, `b`, `c`, `h1`, `h2`): a Python script (standard library only) that patches `shift_screen_b2_mirrored.html` with its overlay script and stylesheet, and writes both pages here. `run.sh` rebuilds them and captures them at 1920x1080 through headless Chrome (`cap.sh`; `CHROME=<path>` picks the browser, and its profile goes to a throwaway folder in `TMPDIR`):

```sh
sh docs/art/mockups/planning_effects_src/gen_h1/run.sh
```

The base page reaches this branch with `main` (#84). Until it is merged in, the generators stop with "shift_screen_b2_mirrored.html comes from main (#84): merge main first".

## Shift scene ambiance (#123, 2026-10-31)

`ambiance_123.html` is the mock-up for #123's first question, the ambiance direction for the shift scene and how it stays clear of the monitor, the receipt and the belt. It relights the game's planning capture (`docs/art/rule_tweaks_2/shift_screen_effects_1280x720.png`) in the title's C+ storefront language: a light story, dither bands on the 640×360 art grid instead of gradients, every colour snapped to the palette (without the v2 brass and velvet), and a time of day shown in a new high window on the back wall. Four looks, one per shift of the week: morning (cool wall, low sun shafts), midday (only the focus falloff), dusk (warm raking light, the lamp on) and night for the Manager's review (the title's teal night, the work floating in its own light, the cold cases glowing). Clear zones (the top bar, the receipt and printer, the monitor, the belt and tickets, the stickers, the hand baskets, the checkout column) are never shaded or lit, and light falls off around them in dither steps; the rewards machine takes one step at most. The page recommends tying the time of day to the shift within the week and building it as one shade field and light list per look in data, and asks five questions (the time-of-day story, how dark the night gets, the window, small motion, the checkout crowd's zone for #120). URL options: `tod=game|morning|midday|dusk|night`, `zones=1`, `snap=0`, `solo=1`. Stills: `ambiance_123_game.png`, `_morning`, `_midday`, `_dusk`, `_night`, `_night_zones` (1280x720 stages) and `_page_top`.

### Re-generating

The source is `ambiance_123_src/` (standard library Python and Playwright): `python build.py` writes the page from `template.html`, embedding the game's fonts and the capture (so its canvas can read the pixels from file://), and `node stills.js` writes every still (`CHROMIUM_PATH` picks the browser).
