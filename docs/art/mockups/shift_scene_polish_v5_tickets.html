Tickets v5: C3's label with a fixed price window and protected icons

This version follows your notes on v4's section C. The base is C3's shelf-edge label. The price sits top-right, so the prices form one row you can scan across the belt. The rule is dark text on a cream plate. The family icons have no seals. Each ticket shows one payout, with no struck price, and the original price and the calculation are in the price's tip.

Each ticket reads in the same order: what the item is (the icons), then what it pays (the window), then what it does (the rule). The price window has the same fixed size on every ticket. Its width is reserved for a realistic large payout, and its contents use a defined fallback for bigger numbers, so the icons and the rule never move. Icons never cover each other: every glyph shows all 9 of its columns. The rule may use 3 lines, and its text is never cut. A rule that still doesn't fit gets a visible MORE chip, with the full text in its tip. GOLDEN, boosted, lowered and 0€ each get their own cue, and the payout states don't rely on hue alone. Every frame is 1920×1080 or a 1:1 sheet, and the backdrop is the game's 1080p capture from v4. The icons are the game's own 9×9 glyphs (art/icons/) at 3 px per art pixel.

The header is split into two fixed zones. The icon label (art 1–22, paper) holds the item's printed families, 1 or 2 side by side, at a 10-art pitch. When an item gains a family (the Price gun's relabel), the label grows a second row for the gained families, the face beside it reads GAINED, and that one ticket grows 10 art (30 px at 1080p). GOLDEN against boosted. GOLDEN is the tier. It gets a 2-art gold ring outside the ink outline, brass on its lower right with a dark rim, and the game's 5×5 sparkle on two corners. The ring sits on the belt, outside the face, so it reads the same on mustard, brown and pale Frozen. Boosted is the payout: a green window with up chevrons (V5a) or an up arrow in ink (V5b), and no gold anywhere. A GOLDEN item that is also boosted shows gold only in its ring. So Tea bags (GOLDEN, 3€) and Milk (plain, 48€) have no cue in common. A lowered payout gets a pale tomato window with down chevrons or a down arrow. 0€ gets a tomato window with paper digits and hatching or a !, and the reason is in its tip.

Hue clashes. Green, pale tomato and tomato are also the Produce, Food and Clearance colours, and no colour left in the palette is free. So each state also gets a texture or a mark, and the window keeps its ink frame. On V5a, the window always sits beside the paper label, which keeps it apart from the face. On V5b, a 0€ window sits right under a Clearance face row, both red. Only the ink rule and the ! separate them there, which is weaker than V5a.

The tips: membership, gained, works-with, price, 0€ and MORE

The tips separate what an item is from what it works with. A membership icon's tip names the family and lists the matching items on the belt, and those tickets light up. A gained icon's tip also says where the family came from (the Price gun, this shift). On the belt frames above, hover a Milk's Dairy icon to see slots 3, 5, 6 and 7 light up. The written chip in a rule says "works with" and lights the items the rule counts. Those items follow the rule's direction and the items' tags, Food and gained families included. Eggs' "×2 next Food" lights Bread, the next Food item, Milk's "+1 per earlier Breakfast" lights every earlier Breakfast item, and a "beside" rule lights its neighbours. When nothing matches, the tip says so, e.g. "No Food item after it on the belt." The price's tip shows the printed price and the calculation. The 0€ tip shows the reason, and the MORE tip shows the full rule.

Every real sticker on V5a, and the check

These are all 59 base cards (coupons included) on V5a, each at its printed price. The page also lays out every one of the 159 cards on both variants without drawing them, the BIG and GOLDEN tiers included. A tier with no sticker text of its own uses its base card's template, filled with the tier's own values, the way CardText.sticker_summary does it (Golden Milk reads "+2 per earlier Breakfast"). A card's tiers share one text size, the one its widest tier needs, so two Milks on a belt always match. Each fit is tested against 96% of the plate, so Godot's text can measure up to 4% wider than Chrome's and still wrap the same way. The check reports any rule that was cut, any line that runs past the plate, any icon that was dropped or covered, any tiers whose text sizes differ, and any price that doesn't fit. It reports them in the console and in the status line at the top.
