Family identities · #128

A decision page for #128, "Prototype distinct gameplay identities for item families". It proposes one belt decision per family, redesigns the five unlocks that repeat Milk's count, and scores every example on this page with a small copy of the game's scoring order. Everything here is a candidate, not an agreed rule. Nothing in the game changes until the questions at the bottom are answered.

1 · The repeat five unlocks play like Milk

Milk counts earlier Breakfast products. Five capsule unlocks give the same rule to another family. The plan each one asks for is the same: draft that family and put this item late. The red line under each ticket is measured in section 4: the share of family-leaning hands where putting it in the last slot, in any order, already earns its best bonus.

2 · Six families, six decisions candidates

Each family is defined by the question it asks while you arrange the belt, not by a number. Families keep some simple items, more than one build each, and bridge items with two tags. Each belt below is scored with the candidate rules on. The highlighted tickets are the family's signature.

3 · First prototype: Produce recommended

Produce is the strongest first family. Its anchor, the Banana chain, is in every starting cart, so all three redesigned unlocks work from the first visit with no later unlock. 4 · Measured: does the order still matter? this page's scorer, seeded hands

For each redesigned item, 120 hands of 6: the item plus 5 products drawn from the pool, with products of the item's family three times as likely. Every order of every hand is scored with today's rule (now) and with the candidate (next). The bar splits the hands by the item's own bonus:

Pool

Best bonus is the item's own bonus at its best, averaged over hands. Item pay and best row are averages in the best order: these are the power check, and a redesign should change the plan without raising them much. Fresh pool: the 20 products a new profile stocks. Expanded: those plus every capsule unlock on this page (today's rules). These are hypotheses checked on a 6-slot toy scorer with no coupons, perks or inspections. The balance simulator (plan section 8) is the real measurement.

6 · Decide with the user

Q1 · The six decisions. Are these the families' signature questions? Breakfast: prepare a big late payout (unchanged, keeping Milk). Produce: one fruit growing, or many different fruits. Bakery: an unbroken batch, ended on purpose. Snacks: small treats spread through the belt, never touching (the opposite of Bakery). Household: tools that serve other families, and pairs. Clearance: turn missed conditions into a limited payout.
Q2 · First family to prototype. A · Produce (recommended): the anchor is in the starting cart, and the chain and the bowl give a real choice. B · Bakery and Snacks together: clump against spread, the clearest contrast, but no Snack is stocked on a fresh profile. C · Clearance: salvage is the most novel verb, but it rewards bad rows and needs the most tuning.
Q3 · The Produce set. Default: Fruit salad (variety), Strawberries (chain into a new fruit), Wonky carrot (chains pass through it). The other Produce unlocks (Grapefruit, Lemon, Bruised apples, Soft tomatoes, Potatoes) stay as they are for the prototype.
Q4 · How we judge it, agreed before building. Default criteria:
  1. After two runs, a player can say in one sentence how Produce changes what they draft or where they put things. Your verdict from a playtest.
  2. Each redesigned ordering item is "last is enough" in at most 30% of hands, on this page's study and in the balance simulator's rows. Fruit salad is judged on drafting instead.
  3. No power creep: in the simulator's greedy rows, the best-row average stays within ±10% of today's at the same quota. Measured, not assumed.
  4. Each one works from a fresh profile: it earns its bonus in at least half of fresh-pool hands, with no other unlock needed.
  5. Collection health: on a fresh and on a full collection, the NEW ITEMS draw offers a usable item for the deck as often as today (--list-gate, the offer guarantee), and unlocking a Produce item never lowers a run's simulated win rate.
Out of scope here (separate issues): badge pins and equipped-pin limits, Double checkout, optional first-trial replacements and named assortments. No wholesale content redesign: section 2's other families are a map for later, not work for this prototype.
