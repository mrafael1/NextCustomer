Seal badge tickets

Direction B's colour card at the taller size, 108×76 screen px (54×38 art px). Each zone answers one question.

  • Family: the seal. Each family the item belongs to gets one round seal, like a price-gun sticker, over the ticket's top-left corner. The seal uses the family colour and has a plain 5×5 symbol on it, with a paper ring and an ink edge. Two or three families fan out to the right, and every symbol stays fully visible.
  • Price: the window. The price sits in one paper window at a fixed spot in the top right, in hard 4×7 digits ("21€"). When the price changes, the old price sits above it in the same window, in small struck grey digits, so "was" and "now" are read together.
  • Effect: the cream plate. The rule is in ink on cream, at most 2 lines. A family named in the rule gets a cap-height colour dot followed by its name in ink. A keyword gets a hard-pixel chip.

The 2-second test. First, the colour: the seal and face register before you read anything, across the whole belt. Second, the price: the brightest box, always in the same spot. Third, the effect: the only running text, on the calmest ground.

Face colour The game colours the face by the family the rule works with (Eggs: Food). The seals show what the item is. "first seal" makes the face match the first seal.

The seal key (3×)

Each symbol is abstract: a sun for Breakfast, an ear of wheat for Bakery, a drop for Dairy, a leaf for Produce, a house for Household, an arrow down for Clearance, a cookie for Snack, a fork and knife for Food (a quiet family that only shows when a rule names it), a snowflake for Frozen and a % for Coupon. The colour does most of the work, and the symbol is there for colour-blind players and for telling Food from Clearance.

Inline targets: a dot plus the word, not a tiny seal and not a coloured word.
  • A seal shrunk to cap height (5 art px) has no room left for a symbol. It becomes a bare colour dot that colour-blind players can't decode, and Food has no seal on the ticket to match it.
  • A coloured word fails on cream. Mustard, Bakery cream, Frozen pale and Dairy light are all under 2:1 against cream, and the shades are muddy at 13 px.
  • So the family name is always written in ink, and a 5×5 dot in the family colour sits in front of it, on the baseline at cap height. The dot uses the same colour as the face (the family the rule works with).

Weak points

  • The face and the seal can disagree. Eggs is a Breakfast item (mustard seal) on a Food face (tomato), because the game colours the face by the family the rule works with. The dot in the rule echoes the face, but the disagreement still has to be learned. The "first seal" toggle removes it, but it changes an approved rule (#105).
  • The seals overhang the ticket by 2 art px on the top and left. They go into the apron above and into the 10-art gap between tickets. Hovering, dragging and the count's flights need to treat the seal as part of the ticket.
  • Three seals fill the whole header (x−2 to x28), so a 4th family or a wider window can't fit. The window is now 15 rows tall to stack "was" over "now", which leaves the plate 18 rows and keeps the rule at 14 px on 2 lines with no slack: a rule that needs 3 lines falls back to 13 or 12 px.
  • The struck old price is small (3×5, grey, no €). It reads as "was" only because of where it sits, above the price, so at 1:1 it is a detail, not something you read at a glance.
  • The symbols are abstract and tiny (5×5). The sun, wheat and cookie need a first-run tooltip. Without colour, Bakery and Snack differ only by their symbol.
  • The rule words are still smooth Atkinson. Only the digits, caps chips, dots and seals are hard pixels, so the resolution mix from v2 is smaller, not gone.
  • Mustard seals on mustard faces (Milk) rely only on the paper ring and the ink edge. They read well at 1:1, but the seal is quieter there than on any other face.
