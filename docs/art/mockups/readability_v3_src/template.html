2Monitor b: the total count-up, juicierDECIDED · b

Monitor b as you chose it, with the count reworked so you can follow every euro into the total, and nothing ever half-drawn. Each step is a beat: the amount flies from its step line (or the item's price, for the base) on a short hop to the left edge of the number and dissolves as it arrives, so it never covers the digits; a multiplier's chip turns from "×2" into the euros it adds ("+5€") halfway. On arrival the number counts up in whole euros, at most three ticks per landing (the printer's three ticks per payout roll, #60), and only the digits that change roll. It punches about its centre: small for a base, more for a bonus, 1.22 for a multiplier (the approved cap), which alone also flashes, frames the number with gold corners and throws two coins off the ladder's ends, down and away, while the status line says "×2 → +5€". Under the number, the ladder lights one lamp per landing, rising like #60's pentatonic ladder (eight lamps, then a brighter octave). WASTED's grey "€0" drops off its step line, with a tomato flicker on the next lamp, no landing. On the belt, #86's rail light runs gold along the rail strip from the giver's ticket to the target's as its effect pays. At 80 % of SPEND the "to go" line pulses. When the total meets SPEND, the climax takes the whole screen (v2's sunburst, stamp and "+N€") with a coin fountain behind the number, then shrinks back into the column, and the top bar fills and plants its flag as today. The top bar and the monitor show the same whole number at every moment, and the receipt prints line by line.

  • Timing: an item's beat is 0.95 s as before; inside it the landings are now at least 0.3 s apart (base at 0.18 s, then each step's amount at 0.48 s and 0.78 s), so Milk's three landings no longer pile up in 0.45 s. The stickers on the belt fire in step with them.
  • Readable, not noisy: one flying amount at a time; the white flash only for a × landing and the climax (v3 flashed every landing, about 12 times in 5.7 s); a base throws no sparks, a "+N" three, a × six; the number never shakes; TOTAL keeps 4 px clear above the digits at the full punch.
  • The price on the monitor is stacked like the ticket's: burst on top (22 px), struck old price under it (15 px), in the item column's top-right corner.

3Sticker stacks: 1 to 4, then a foldQ2

The polished stickers and giver badges are unchanged. An item still carries every sticker it earns: the stack sits on the item's right shoulder (so the item stays visible) and grows upward, the newest at the bottom, 1 to 4 in full, overlapping by only 3 px. From the 5th, the oldest fold away so the stack never gets taller than four. Hover a folded stack to fan it out (every sticker in step order, numbered, on a short arc above the item). During the count the stack fires in order; the fold fans out by itself while its stickers fire, then closes. Which fold?

  • A · "+N" chip. A grey chip on top of the stack with a card-fan mark and the count ("+3") in ink. It punches each time another sticker folds into it.
  • B · Pile edges. No chip: the folded stickers peek out above the top sticker as up to three greyed edges, with a small ink count disc ("3") on the pile.
Recommended: A. The chip is one clear object with a number, the same size and grey as the WASTED and fold labels players already know; B's edges read as "more stuff" but at game size they merge with the sticker tilt, and the count disc is a new small thing to learn.

Stacks of 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7 at game size (hover the 5 and the 7 to fan them)

Building up while you plan, then firing in order in the count (loops every 8.75 s: seven stickers land one by one, the fold takes the 5th to 7th, the cursor fans it, then the count fires all seven; game size and 2×)

  • Which ones fold: the oldest (the top of the stack, the first to fire). The four you see are the newest, the ones your last moves added.
  • The fan: all stickers in step order, in rows of at most four on a gentle arch just above the item (the oldest row on top, read left to right), each tilted by its place and carrying its order number, 1 to N. It stays above the belt and within about one slot either side (v3's 150° arc reached down to the neighbours' tickets). A single 100° arc of radius 110 was tried first, but seven stickers on it overlap by half and hide their amounts, so it became two rows. It opens in 0.18 s and closes when the pointer leaves.
  • The price climbs on the ticket as each sticker fires (here to €170: a 3-digit price in the wide burst, with the struck €4 still under it).

4Highlight to help mixingQ3

While you plan: hover a family glyph (on a ticket, the monitor or the Cart) and every item of that family on the belt, in the hand and in the Cart stays lit while the rest greys out. Hover (or hold) an item and the items it works with stay lit: the ones its rule names, and the ones whose rule names it. A small green check marks a pair that already pays in this order; a tomato "!" marks a penalty (Soup pays €0 beside Frozen). The grey is calm: desaturated to 20 % and dimmed to 78 %, nothing glows. The stage runs an auto-demo (Produce glyph, Breakfast glyph, Milk, Cereal in the hand, Frozen peas); move the pointer over it to take over.

  • A · Rule matches everywhere (recommended): what the rule could work with, on the belt, in the hand and in the Cart, plus the check on pairs that already pay. Helps you pick what to bring in from the hand.
  • B · Pays now only: the #71 EffectGraph links (presentation/effect_graph.gd, related_slots) on the belt; nothing in the hand.
Recommended: A. B only repeats what the effect markers already show for this order; mixing is about what to put where next, which A answers. The game needs one small static matcher for A (a rule's target against a card's tags or id); EffectGraph still supplies the checks.
  • Glyph hover uses the card's families (tags), the same as the glyphs on its ticket; the padlock lights the staples. Item hover reads the rules: Milk lights the Breakfast items it counts, Eggs with its check (its "×2 next " pays Milk now) and Cereal ("+3 beside Milk"); Frozen peas finds no Frozen partner (that is why it WASTES): it lights Strawberries ("+3 beside ", and the peas are Produce too), Lemon with its check (it pays them now) and Soup with its penalty; the Eggs ("×2 next ") only get a faint dashed outline.
  • Food targets are faint (v3 review: Eggs lit up on almost every hover): a rule that names Food matches nearly every product, so such a match shows as a faint dashed outline over the grey, unless the pair pays now (then it is lit with its check). The same goes for Mixed herbs and the Carrier bag.
  • The hovered item keeps its pointer brackets; the tooltip on a hand item shows its name and short rule, because the hand has no tickets.
  • Not a new mode: it only shows while the pointer rests (Godot's usual tooltip delay) and clears the moment you move off, drag or scan.
