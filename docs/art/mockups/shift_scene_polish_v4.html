Shift scene polish, v4: 1920×1080 first

This version answers your feedback on the NC-polish build and on v3's tickets. Every stage below is a full 1920×1080 frame. Its backdrop is the game itself, captured at 1080p (euro-after.png), and only the parts under review are redrawn over it with the game's own sprites, palette and fonts. Art pixels are 3 screen pixels, as in the game at 1080p. Use the 1:1 button on a 1080p screen to see the frames at their real size.

A covers the left zone: a wider gauge that refills in a new colour on each lap, no flag, and a column that reads as one built piece. B covers what sits behind a short receipt. C shows the tickets with seal badges made from the current family icons, and no old price. D is the legibility strip, with today's sizes against the proposed ones. The belt's quota light stays exactly as built, minus its flag.

A1

Steel tower, glass gauge in a housing, MET lamp and brass plate

RECOMMENDED

The gauge sits in a steel housing with an ink outline, lit on the left and shaded on the right, and rivets in its caps. The glass is 39 px wide at 1080p (13 art px), up from 30, and the housing is 57 px wide, up from 39. It has tick marks at ¼, ½ and ¾ and a highlight down its left side. The red target line is level with the top of the big 80€. Above the housing is a round lamp, which is the lap badge. It stays dark until the quota is met, then lights green with a ✓. At each lap it turns that lap's colour and reads ×2, ×3 and so on. Below the housing, a brass plate with an engraved € fills the dead corner beside the printer. The tower gets a trim on its top and left edges, rivets on its right strip and a recessed well around the receipt, so the column reads as one machine. The receipt doesn't move.

A2

Brass thermometer: widest glass, a bulb, an enamel lap tag

The frame is a thin brass one, so the glass is the widest of the three: 45 px (15 art), +50%. The tube ends in a round bulb that fills the dead space under it and always holds the current lap's colour, so the gauge reads as a thermometer before it fills. Ticks run every 10%. The lap badge is an enamel tag that hangs from a hook at the top and swings a little when it changes. It's warmer and more shop-fitting, closer to the machine's brass than to the register's steel.

A3

Pillar: the glass runs down past the printer to the CHECKOUT panel

A1's steel housing, carried down past the printer to the CHECKOUT panel. The glass is 35% taller (513 px), so every euro moves the level further, and the column's left edge becomes one unbroken pillar from the top of the screen to the keys. The housing covers the printer's plain left end (12 px of steel, no light or key). The cost: the printer box needs a shorter left cheek, and the gauge's top is no longer the only thing level with the 80€.

Verdict: A1. It's 30% wider and much more present (57 px of housing, against today's 39 px tube), and nothing else on screen moves. It also fills every dead patch of the column: the lamp above the gauge, the plate below it, the trim and the well. A2 is the widest and the most charming, but its brass fights the register's steel and its bulb puts the gauge's base level with the printer instead of the receipt's foot. A3 is the boldest read during the count, but it needs a printer redraw.

Laps, all variants: the tube never re-scales any more. Each lap is exactly one quota. When a lap completes, the glass flashes and the next colour pours in from the bottom over the full previous one. The previous colour stays visible above the new surface, so you can see how far into the lap you are. The lamp or tag pops with the new multiple. The flag is gone from the gauge and from the belt (the belt's fill is kept as built). Each gain still sloshes, sprays and bubbles as today.

Touches ui/quota_meter.gd (no re-scale; LAP_RAMPS green, gold, orange, violet, teal, cycling, with orange and violet added to art/src/palette.txt and Palette; the lap pour; no QuotaFlag), ui/quota_flag.gd (removed, or kept only for the belt's crossing if you want it back), ui/quota_light.gd and ui/quota_crossing.gd (no flag), ui/register_tower.gd and art/frames/register_tower.png (trim, rivets, well), a new art/src/scenery/gauge_housing.txt (housing, caps, lamp bezel, € plate), and ShiftStage.TUBE_RECT 8,50,24,244 → 2,40,38,266 (stage px).

B · A short receipt: what sits above it

Today the receipt sits on the printer and grows upwards, so a short one leaves the top of the well empty dark grey. Each frame below plays a planning phase from 0 to 7 items, one line every 0.7 s, then holds. They all use A1's column, and the gauge follows the projected total.

B1

A pegboard backboard with the store's plate

The well becomes a perforated steel backboard, the kind a register's back panel has, with a riveted NEXT CUSTOMER plate and the cart mark near its top. The receipt rises in front of it and covers the plate once it passes 5 items. It's simple and honest, but at 0 to 4 items the plate is the loudest thing in the column.

B2

A full-length slip from the start: one row per belt slot

RECOMMENDED

The receipt is always full length. TODAY'S TARGET is fixed at the top and the foot is fixed at the bottom, and in between is one row per belt slot. Empty slots print the game's own placeholder, "5 ·····" (ReceiptView.EMPTY_SLOT_TEXT), in grey. Placing an item turns its row into the line, with a short ink-in. Nothing in the column moves, and there is never any dark well. The rows also tell you how many slots are still empty. With more lines than rows (in the count), it scrolls as today.

B3

The printer feeds it: a smoked paper chute above the receipt

The receipt stays bottom-anchored, and each new line feeds the whole slip up out of the printer, with a short slide and the roll turning. Above it, the well is a paper chute: smoked acrylic between two steel guide rails, with paper-path grooves and a printed ▲ TEAR mark. The empty space now reads as part of the printer. It has the most motion of the three, and the top of the receipt moves on every line.

B2 at D's sizes, with 6, 7 and 9 belt slots (v4.1)

The belt has 7 slots, 6 with Short belt, and up to 9 with Big basket and the Extra till inspection together. This frame applies D's sizes everywhere: TARGET digits 72 px, receipt lines 21 px and the foot at 21 to 39 px. The paper stays 491 px tall, with TODAY'S TARGET fixed at the top (123 px) and the foot fixed at the bottom (120 px). To make room, the two header lines become one: SHIFT 5/12 · 7 ITEMS. "NEXT CUSTOMER · REG 3" is no longer printed (see the questions). The rows share what's left:

  • 6 slots: 30 px rows, with 10 px of paper spare above the foot.
  • 7 slots: 27 px rows. The 24 px icons have 3 px between them.
  • 9 slots: the info line is dropped too, giving 24 px rows. The 24 px icon boxes touch, but the drawings keep a gap and the 21 px text still fits. Nothing scrolls in planning, and nothing in the column moves. A 10th slot isn't possible today; if one is ever added, the lines step down to 19.5 px.

Verdict: B2. It fixes the cause rather than decorating the gap: there's no short receipt any more, only empty rows. TODAY'S TARGET never moves, the foot never moves, and the receipt reads top to bottom in belt order. B3 is the best if you want the printer to feel alive in planning, and B1 is the cheapest.

B2 touches presentation/receipt_view.gd (in planning, pad to the belt's slot count with EMPTY_SLOT_TEXT rows and pin the foot to the bottom; a full-height paper even when short) and ui/shift_stage.gd (the receipt's rect is fixed at full height). The count's receipt is unchanged.

C · Tickets: seal badges with the current icons, no old price

All tickets are 162×114 at 1080p (108×76 stage px, 54×38 art px at 3 screen px). The family icons are the game's current 9×9 glyphs (art/icons/family_*.png, aisle_*.png) at their real size, 27 px. The struck old price is gone on every variant, so the price window holds one bold 6×8 number in "N€". Its colour still says boosted (mustard), lowered (tomato) or 0€ (red with paper digits). The rule text is Atkinson 800 at 21 px, falling back to 19.5 and then 18 px, never smaller. The row is the real belt, matching the items and the receipt above it: Eggs, golden Bread, Milk on the 0€ spot check, the wasted Multipack coupon, then Milk at 21€, 48€ and 63€. The keyword rule (Birthday candles) and the long rule (Bent lolly) are in the stress row below. A ticket with no rule (Bread) prints its name in ink on the plate. The tickets push the baskets 18 px lower, as in v3.

C1

Seal badge, as you asked

Each family the item belongs to gets a round seal (15 art, 45 px) over the ticket's top-left corner. Each seal has an ink rim, a ring in the family's colour (lit top-left) and the current glyph at 1:1 inside it. A second family overlaps 10 art to the right, so two seals end before the window. The face is the colour of the family the item works with (#105). The cream plate fills everything under the window, so a rule has room for 3 lines at 18 px. A target family is the seal concept's 5×5 dot plus the word.

C2

Seal badge + v3's recommended grafts

RECOMMENDED

The same seals, with v3's recommended ticket under them. The price window is clipped into the top-right corner. The rule is on the cream plate, a target family is a word chip (a small piece of that family's strip, with the word in it), and keywords use the game's KeywordChip. The name strip along the foot spells out the item's families (BAKERY | BRKFST). So you learn the icons from the words, and the strip still reads for colour-blind players. The rule keeps 2 lines at 21 px.

C3

My alternative: a shelf-edge label (icons and price in one header row)

The top of the ticket is a price label, like a shelf-edge label. It runs the full width, with the family glyphs on its left (1 art of margin, sitting on the same baseline as the digits) and the price on its right. Its fill takes the price's state. Nothing hangs past the ticket's edge, so there are no "coins". Three icons fit next to a 2-digit price. The works-with colour frames the plate on all sides, and the plate takes the rest, for 3 lines.

Stress specimens at 1:1 (3 families + 128€, a fresh coupon, a wasted keyword, the Frozen pale face, the dark Household face, a keyword rule, a long rule on the dark Snack face)

Verdict: C2. The seals give you the icons as you asked, at their real size and fully legible. They're the game's own glyphs, so they also match the receipt, the tips and the deck. The grafts fix what the judges disliked about the seal concept: the target is a family word chip, not a dot that blurs at 1:1, and the window is the bold corner one. The strip keeps the family names in words, which a new player needs until the icons are learnt. Dropping the old price frees the window's top half, so the number gets the full 6×8 size.

  • C1 has the most room for the rule (3 lines), but its dot targets are the weakest part of the seal concept, and without the strip a family is shown only by its icon.
  • C3 is the tidiest: one header row, nothing overhanging, and three icons fit. But the face colour shrinks to a frame, which weakens #105's "works with" read, and the price label is no longer the corner window the pay flights and burst use (they'd move 2 to 4 px).
  • Three families (the stress row): the seals stop at 2, because a 3rd would run into the window. C2's strip then shows three colour segments with no words, and the full names are in the tip. There are no more 4-letter codes like HHLD (v4.1). With 1 or 2 families the strip spells them out (BAKERY | BRKFST). In C1 the 3rd family is only in the tip. C3 fits 3 icons next to a 2-digit price, but next to 128€ it shows 2.
