Start screen v3: YOUR STORE, then YOUR BUILD

You liked v2's three parts: aisle completion, badge pins and pick a starter pack. Your note was that it put too much on one screen. The screen has to be clear and quick to read without losing the player. Round 3 splits it into two pages, each with one obvious next action, fewer and bigger pieces, and room around them.

Page 1, YOUR STORE, is the store-side setup, and most of it is saved to your profile: today's stock (aisles with n/m and ★, the four states, RESTOCK or CHOOSE AISLES, the next goal), today's deal and your 4 staples with their swap chips. The big tomato NEXT key (Enter) leads on, and ◀ TITLE (or Esc) goes back to the title menu. Page 2, YOUR BUILD, holds the choice you make each run: pick a starter pack, badge pins, and the full cart of 15 slots with its family counts. OPEN STORE (Enter) starts the run, and ◀ STORE goes back with your choices kept.

What moved, and why. The deal moved to page 1 because RESTOCK changes it. On the same page, you see that these aisles deal these 6, and a RESTOCK re-deals them in front of you. The pins moved to page 2 because, like the pack, they are a choice for this build. Page 1 is shown every time, even on a new save. There it takes a few seconds: a look at the aisles and the next goal. Enter, Enter goes through both pages.

Try it: use PAGE and PROFILE in the toolbar, or play through the frame. On page 1, hover a stock row to light the cards it deals. Click a staple with SWAP › to open the picker. From 3 completed aisles, try RESTOCK ›. Then press NEXT. On page 2, hover a pack to preview it in the cart, click one or press 1 to 3, click the badge to change pins, and press OPEN STORE. Esc closes a panel; with no panel open it goes back one step (page 2 → page 1 → the title). The look follows the shift scene, the title's menu plates and the toy machine. Teal plates are secondary actions and the tomato plate is the one primary action. Words are hard Pixelify pixels, numbers are the chunky price-gun digits, and sentences are Atkinson. The frames are pixel 9-slices built from art/src/frames, and every colour comes from palette.txt (greyed states use palette greys and the machine's 62% rubber-dark dim, never a CSS filter). Behind the panels is the dim store: StoreArt's back shelving, with recessed spot lamps whose dithered StorefrontArt cones light the one area that matters, the deal on page 1 and the packs on page 2 (the picked pack's lamp burns hot).

The two pages

1

YOUR STORE (page 1)

Left: TODAY'S STOCK. The rows are sorted by what they do today. First come the three that deal (the Staples shelf, then the 2 aisles, all three marked DEALT FROM), then the NOT DEALT aisle, which the one line above the list spells out ("in stock, but only as machine prizes"). A mustard rule and a NOT IN STOCK TODAY label then split off NOT TODAY and the locked aisles, whose pill gives the way in: a padlock and OPENS AT 4. An ITEMS column head sits over n/m (items you own of the aisle's items, ★ when complete), so 5/7 on a new save doesn't read as progress you made. The three dealing rows feed a green source rail (the shift scene's rail light): a stub from each row, a bus in the gap, and a rail under the deal that lights under each group. RESTOCK › (3–5 ★) or CHOOSE AISLES › (6 ★) sits in the panel's header. Below it, the NEXT GOAL slip is a 6-stamp track: ★1 swap, ★3 RESTOCK, ★4 every staple, ★6 choose the pair.

Right, top: TODAY'S DEAL. The 6 cards are grouped under the sign of the shelf or aisle they came from, in the same colour, glyph and name as the stock row, standing on the source rail. When three full names don't fit, the tiles step down from 162 to 150 px, then the Staples shelf group keeps its sign alone. Hovering a row lights its stub, its rail segment and its cards; the rest go dark. Prices sit in the V5a ticket's paper window, the family colour only in the glyphs and a thin strip at the window's foot, so no price looks boosted or lowered. A RESTOCK drops the new 6 in one by one, and the FIRST DEAL / RESTOCKED switch sits in this header (the stock header then just says RESTOCK USED). Right, bottom: YOUR STAPLES. The 4 slots have chips as wide as their tile (SWAP ›, WAS + the old staple's mini, NO SWAP) and a SWAPS meter (used/open). On a new save there are no chips and no meter, only one lock strip under the four, aligned with them.

Counter: a ribbed belt strip over the rubber, ◀ TITLE (teal plate) on its own riveted steel plaque at the left, keycap hints (ENTER, ESC), and the tomato NEXT key on a matching plaque at the right. The key rests on the title's tomato face; the lit face and the price-gun tick show only while it holds the marker (hover).

2

YOUR BUILD (page 2)

Top: PICK A STARTER PACK, under three spot lamps. Three wide pack cards: the name big in the family colour, the key badge, the 3 items, and the family glyph with the hint. Hover to preview a pack in the cart, then click it or press 1–3. The picked pack keeps its place (only the frame changes, so the hints stay on one line), its lamp burns hot, and a gold PICKED price-gun sticker slaps on at -4° (the machine's 1.5 → 1 back-ease, a "tag" sound in the game). GOOD FIRST PICK is a mustard-pale shelf talker, not green (green is for paid and boosted).

Bottom left: YOUR CART n/15. The full recap, one slot per card. The STAPLES 4, YOUR PACK 3 and PIN CARDS slots form one row, and TODAY'S DEAL 6 with BY FAMILY forms the other; PIN CARDS and BY FAMILY share one column edge. BY FAMILY names the top 3 families (the rest are in the cards' tips), with the note that a card can count twice. Before a pick, the equation leaves the pack out and adds "+3 when you pick a pack". Bottom right: BADGE PINS. Click the badge or the pin text to change pins. A pin's cards show up in the cart at once. The pins on the badge are drawn glyphs (recycle arrows, scissors, a basket…); ×2 stays two till letters.

Counter: ◀ STORE (teal plate on its riveted plaque, like ◀ TITLE on page 1) on the left and OPEN STORE › on the right. Before a pack is picked, it reads PICK A PACK FIRST, greyed on the steel face but in ink, and the packs' 1 2 3 keys blink mustard while the pack header says "Pick one to open the store".

⇄

Both pages, and the move between them

Top bar: OPENING TIME · SHIFT 1/12, the six aisle signs with n/6 ★, the seed, and Cart n/15. Step rail: the 1 YOUR STORE › 2 YOUR BUILD tabs. The current tab is a paper chip in a mustard selection frame (no tick: the tick is the one marker, and it belongs to the counter key), and a done step gets a green check. The rail has no explanation line beside it (the counter says what is saved, once). Tab 1 keeps the check's room on both pages, so the rail never moves. The tabs can be clicked too.

Unlocked aisles that weren't drawn read NOT TODAY.DECIDED
Each dealt aisle dealsEach of the 2 DEALT FROM aisles gives at least 1 of the 6. The rest is drawn from the whole pool as today. Without this rule, a uniform draw can deal 0 from an aisle (v2's stand-in dealt no Bakery at 3 ★), and page 1 would then show a green DEALT FROM sign with no cards under it. The JS stand-in here follows the rule.PROPOSAL
Stock row orderRows sort by today's state: Staples shelf, DEALT FROM (in draw order), NOT DEALT, NOT TODAY, then LOCKED (each group in data order). This puts the deal's three sources beside the deal.NEW
Staple swapsEach completed aisle opens one swap, up to the 4 staples. A swap takes the plain tier of any collected product of a completed aisle (capsules allowed; no BIG or GOLDEN, no coupons), with at most 2 of a card in the cart and at most 2 swapped staples from one aisle. Swaps are saved to the profile, changed on page 1, and never re-deal.DECIDED
RESTOCK (3–5 ★)Once a run, on page 1. Take out a dealt aisle and put in an unlocked aisle that isn't in today's stock; the NOT DEALT aisle stays. You choose blind. The new 6 come from starting_deck_restock (the same choice gives the same 6) and are saved at once. You can switch between FIRST DEAL and RESTOCKED. The packs never change.DECIDED
CHOOSE AISLES (6 ★)Pick the 2 dealt aisles. The pair is saved to the profile and is dealt from in the next runs too. It uses the same once-a-run token.DECIDED (default)
Pins on page 2Click the badge or the pin text to open BADGE PINS. The choice is saved to the profile and worn by the waiting run. A pin's cards show up in the cart's PIN CARDS slots and count toward 15 (4 + 3 + 6 = 13 leaves 2 slots, so today's pins can't overfill it; a WEAR "cart full" state is needed only if a pin ever brings 3+ cards). A pin change never re-deals.DECIDED
Cart on page 1Page 1 has no cart recap. Its top-bar Cart counts what page 1 makes, plus pin cards: staples + deal (10/15, or 11/15 with a card pin), never the pack, even a pre-picked GOOD FIRST PICK the player hasn't seen. On page 2 the pack joins (13/15).PROPOSAL
NOT DEALT and LOCKED on screenNOT DEALT is spelled out above the list ("in stock, but only as machine prizes"). A locked aisle's pill reads OPENS AT 4 with the padlock (aisle_listable_min). The RESTOCK sheet lists only the 2 dealt aisles under TAKE OUT; the NOT DEALT aisle is a note ("Household stays"), never a padlocked chip.NEW

Open questions for you

  1. Q1 · Rename the 4 "staples"? Page 1 now shows the Staples shelf (6 products in the deal's pool) and YOUR STAPLES (the 4 fixed cards) side by side, so the clash is easier to see than in v2. A name like "regulars" for the 4 would fix it.
  2. Q2 · Run 1 on a new save: is the dealt pair seeded (as shown) or fixed to Cold cases + Pantry?
  3. Q3 · The 5th completed aisle still gives nothing new: the goal track's stamp 5 has no label. Should it give something (a 2nd RESTOCK, or the pair choice at 5)?
  4. Q4 · Names on the card faces: page 1's tiles show the sprite, family glyphs and price (V5a's "icon → price → rule"), with the name in the tip. Is that enough for a new player, or do you want a name line under each tile? It costs 27 px per row.

Defaults I took (say if any is wrong): Esc goes back one step (page 2 → page 1 → the title); the page isn't saved (resume opens page 1); 1–3 work on page 2 only; page 1's Cart counts staples + deal + pin cards, the pack joins on page 2; the swap fair-deal warning stays off (v2 Q5, still open as a default).
