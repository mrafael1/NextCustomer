Ticket: band + word

This is ticket B (colour card, fixed price window, rule on a cream plate) at the taller 54×38 art px (108×76 on screen). It answers "the icons feel odd" by taking every item-like icon off the ticket. A family is now a colour band with its name in hard-pixel caps, and a target family inside a rule is a small piece of that same band, a word chip. Every number and every capital letter is drawn from pixel sheets at 2×. Only the rule's words are Atkinson.

How each part answers family, price and effect

  • Band (family). The top 7 rows are the family's colour, with its name in 3×5 capitals. You read "DAIRY" there; you don't have to decode a milk carton. With 2 or 3 families the band splits into segments, and each segment keeps its own name, shortened only when the names don't fit.
  • Symbol (family, optional). A 5×5 single-colour shape (a drop, a dome, a sun ring) that is exactly as tall as the capitals, so it reads as part of the word. It appears only on a single-family band.
  • Price window (price). A fixed inset box, right side, rows 8–18, in big 4×7 digits with the euro after the number ("128€"). Its fill gives the state: paper is the base price, pale gold is boosted, tomato is 0€.
  • Was-tab (price). When the shown price isn't the base price, the old one sits on a small cream tab left of the window, slashed in tomato. It reads like the "was" on a real sale label, and its euro is redrawn so it no longer looks like an E.
  • Cream plate (effect). The rule, in Atkinson 13 px ink, 2 lines. A target family is a band chip ("BREAKFAST" on mustard). A keyword (BATCH, WASTED…) is a dark ink chip, so a family and a mechanic never look alike.

The 2-second test: on a belt row you read the price window first (biggest, highest-contrast digits, same place on every ticket), then the band (colour at a glance, the word when you look), and the plate third (the rule, with the family chip its colour-matched anchor). When a chip's colour matches another ticket's band, the "works with" link is visible without reading.

A Golden Milk with a Price-gun family (Household), so the band holds 3 segments. Brass outline, a gold top row, a corner sparkle. The stress rule follows the brief (+1, was 4€); the real Golden Milk is 6€ with +2, which is the same width.

(c) Coupons

A coupon's band always says COUPON (teal, perforated under the band). Its window is the stub (scissors, never a € amount). A family coupon names its family as a chip in the rule instead of borrowing the family's face. A fizzle greys and strikes the stub.

(d) Keyword chips

Keywords are dark ink chips with capitals in the keyword's calm colour (BATCH is Bakery cream, as in KeywordChip). A wrong job (the WASTED state) turns the chip tomato and adds a cross after the word, as KeywordChip.set_wasted does. Family chips are always light pieces of a band, so the two kinds never get confused.

(e) The 0€ Spot check slot

The ticket owns the amount: a tomato window says 0€ and the was-tab keeps the lost 4€ visible. The chip under it owns the cause: a warning sign only, with no second 0€. On an empty slot, the full talker is the only 0€.

Band specimen: every family, the splits and the short names

The fitting rule: try every full name. While the names don't fit the band, shorten the longest one to its next form (full, then short, then a 4-letter code). The capital I is 1 px wide, M and W are 5, N is 4, and all other letters are 3.

Zones (art px, 54×38, drawn at 2×)

  • Body x0–53 × y0–37: 1 px ink outline (brass-dark on GOLDEN), 2 px die-cut corners, a 35% ink shadow at (1,1), a shade column at x52 and a shade row at y36. The face is the first band family's colour; a coupon's is paper.
  • Band y1–7, x1–52: light row y1, face y2–6, shade row y7. Capitals 3×5 on y2–6 start at x3 (x10 after a 5×5 symbol at x3). Splits are segments sized to their names, joined by a 1 px ink-light seam. Coupons have a dashed perforation on y7.
  • Price window x31–53 × y8–18: ink outline on x31, y8 and y18, plus the ticket's own right edge. Inside it is x32–52 × y9–17 with a 1 px highlight on its top and left. The digits are 4×7 ("1" is 3), right-aligned at x51, rows y10–16. "128€" is 18 px wide in 21 px of room.
  • Was-tab right edge x29, rows y9–17: cream, with a cream-shade bottom row. The digits are 3×5 grey-dark, right-aligned at x28, rows y11–15, with a 1 px tomato slash from bottom left (y16) to top right (y11), 1 px past each end, so the middle row of the digits stays readable.
  • Plate x2–51 × y20–35: cream, with a cream-shade inset on y20 and x2. The rule is Atkinson 800 13 px (12 px fallback, never 3 lines), with baselines at screen y54 and y68, from screen x8 to x100.
  • Chips, 7 rows tall (screen baseline −12 to +2). A family chip is a light row, face rows with ink or paper capitals, and a shade row, 2 px of padding, cut corners. A keyword chip is ink with capitals in the calm colour; a WASTED one is tomato with paper capitals and a 5×5 cross.
  • Cause chip (Spot check): 13×13, tomato, the 2× warning sign, centred under the ticket and overlapping its bottom by 1 art px.

Weak points

  • The face colour changes meaning. Today it is the family the card works with (#105), so every Milk is mustard. Here it is what the item is, so Milk turns teal, and "works with" moves to the mustard BREAKFAST chip in its rule. The belt row changes colour.
  • With 3 families the band falls back to 4-letter codes (DAIR, HHLD, BRKF). They are readable, but they are codes. Shortened names need their tooltip, or a reader learns them over time.
  • A 3×5 capital is 10 screen px tall. At 1:1 it is crisp but small, and long names (BREAKFAST, CLEARANCE) are dense. The words are smaller than the rule's Atkinson.
  • On a single-family ticket with no struck price, the cell left of the window (x1–30, y8–18) is empty face. It's breathing room, but it's also unused room.
  • The window sits 6 rows lower than in v2's B, so the count's pay flights land 12 screen px lower. That's code work in PlanningLayer and pay_burst.
  • Chips are as tall as the line pitch (14 px), so a chip on line 1 and another on line 2 touch. Rules rarely have two chips, but they would read as one block.
  • Light families (Frozen, Bakery) have low contrast between the cream was-tab and the face.
