1Family icons: one set everywhere

One icon per tag (the 9 tags rules point at), built from the aisle glyphs and sign colours, plus two marks (Coupon, Staple). The same icon shows on the shelf ticket, inside effect text, on the monitor and in the Cart. Which look, and do tag words stay inside the effect text?

  • A · Object glyphs. Full-colour objects like today's aisle glyphs (Food reuses the Pantry tin). 18 px. Shape carries the meaning; colour is secondary. Icons only in the effect text.
  • B · Sign tiles. A paper shape on a tile in the family's sign colour, like the aisle sign boards: 18 px (9×9 art, the shape uses the full 7×7 inside the outline) inline, 22 px (11×11) on the monitor. Icons only in the effect text.
  • C · Sign tiles + word in the effect. B's tiles, with the tag word after the icon inside the effect text ("×2 next [tile] Produce"). The ticket's tag row stays icons only. Longer effect lines (two lines on most tickets).
Recommended: C. There is no tutorial, and "×2 next [tile]" can't be read until you've learned the tiles; the word teaches the tile every time you read an effect. At game size (1×) colour alone doesn't separate the teals (Dairy, Frozen, Coupon) or the browns (Food, Bakery, Snack), so the shape and the word have to carry it. If players learn the tiles fast, dropping the words later (B) is a one-line change.

The set at game size: 18 px (9×9) and 22 px (11×11) at 1×, then 2× for review

  • Mapping: Produce = the apple (Fruit & veg), Bakery = the loaf, Snack = the crisp packet, Household = the spray bottle, Frozen = the snowflake (Cold cases): the same shapes as the aisle glyphs players already see on the signs. New: Food (fork and knife, neutral cream), Breakfast (rising sun), Dairy (milk bottle), Clearance (%). Coupon = scissors on the coupon teal, Staple = the padlock the Cart already uses.
  • Pixel fix since the first draft: the 18 px tile dropped its 1 px bevel, so the shape gets the full 7×7 (the same shapes as the 22 px tile). The old 5×5 shapes read as faces, "E", "/", "X" and "YI".
  • Tomato stays reserved for fizzles, CHECKOUT and CLOSED, so Breakfast moved from tomato light to the wood orange (light sun on orange; Bakery is a dark loaf on tan). Clearance uses the velvet. Mustard/gold stays "paid / multiplier".
  • The aisle signs in the store would show the tile of their main tag (Cold cases has both Dairy and Frozen products: it keeps its snowflake sign).

2Item tickets under the items (and on the Cart's shelves)

Today: a plain white ticket with a mustard band, the € base, one aisle glyph and the sticker text. New: the ticket carries its family colour and its tag icons (so you can see Milk counts as Breakfast), the price uses the checkout's € tag (gold when the plan pays more), and the effect is bold. The same ticket hangs on the Cart's shelf rail. Which ticket?

  • A · Colour header. A header band in the item's main family colour holds the € tag and the tag icons; the effect sits below on paper.
  • B · Colour spine. A family-colour spine down the left with the tag icons stacked; € and effect on paper to its right. Effect lines are narrower.
  • C · Colour card. The whole ticket in the family colour with the effect on it; € tag and icons on a paper strip at the bottom, like a shelf-edge label. Most colourful, busiest.
Recommended: A. Colour and icons sit on one band you can scan along the rail, and the effect keeps the full ticket width (the stress row below is where B runs out of room).
  • Food is left out of the tag row. Almost every product is Food, so the tile told the player nothing; Food shows only where a rule names it ("×2 next [Food]") and in the Cart's tag counts. Tickets now carry 1–2 icons.
  • Main family (header colour) = the item's most specific tag, in this order: Frozen, Dairy, Produce, Bakery, Snack, Household, Clearance, Breakfast, Food. Milk → Dairy, Eggs → Breakfast, Frozen peas → Frozen. Risk: a one-family colour says "Frozen peas is Frozen", yet Lemon's "×2 next Produce" hits it, because it is Produce too. The tag icons in the header carry the full set; the colour only names one of them.
  • The ticket shows one price: the planned payout. The struck base price and the separate gold PayTag burst are folded into the € tag (gold = pays more than its base). The base price still shows on the monitor ("base €3").
  • Coupons keep the coupon teal with the scissors mark and no tag icons (stress row, first ticket).
  • "The shelf": the brief's "effects shown on the shelf" most likely means the Cart's shelves (the deck view is "a shelf … stickers on a steel price rail"), shown above in each ticket style. The belt tickets already use the short sticker text today. Three places still print the long rule_text: the scan popup, the monitor and the machine's prize tickets. The monitor gets the short text in this pass; open question: do the scan popup and the prize tickets switch too, or keep the long text as the one place a player can read the full rule?

3Font of the scanned item's name

The first draft set the whole monitor text in each candidate, but at larger sizes than A, so B and C overflowed by construction, and Chrome faked their bold. This round is fair: the body text is Atkinson Hyperlegible Next in every option (tags 14 px bold, effect 16 px weight 800, steps 14 px bold), because the effect needs a real bold and only Atkinson has one. Only the item's name changes face, each at the same cap height, real weights only. Which face for the name?

  • NOW · current fonts and sizes in the new layout: Pixelify Sans 20 name, Atkinson 12–13 regular text.
  • A · Pixelify Sans (the game's display face), name at 22 px, semi-bold.
  • B · Jersey 15 (Google Fonts, OFL): tall, condensed; fits long names.
  • C · VT323 (Google Fonts, OFL): a terminal/LCD face; reads as "a screen".
Recommended: A. It matches the signs, stamps and buttons, and with the body in Atkinson the name is the only pixel text that has to be read, at a size where Pixelify is clear. Pixelify does misread when it is small: the bay's 12 px uppercase label read "FRO2EN PEAS" and the first draft's legend headers read "8 · Sign tile" and "0 · Tile + word". So small labels move to Atkinson in every option.

    4Monitor: one screen, no switching

    The monitor stops cycling the shift-brief / loyalty / coupon slides. It always shows the item under the scanner (NEXT = belt slot 1 when nothing is hovered) and the SPEND gauge. CHECKOUT doesn't swap the screen any more: the same item layout counts each product in turn. The gauge is the approved K1 "one tank": the big number and its € are the tank (one liquid level behind both, "28€" with a plain gold € after the number). In planning it shows a hatched ghost up to the planned total; during the count real liquid rises, holds just under the brim until the quota lands, then fills, and the sunburst and SPEND MET stamp fire. Where does the K1 number sit?

    • A · Band under the text. The K1 number on the right of a band along the bottom of the item side, SPEND €26+ on its left. The bay keeps its place; the item text gets the full width above the band.
    • B · Column on the right. The K1 number stacked in its own column with SPEND €26+ under it. The item text column is narrower but taller.
    • C · No bay. The teal bay goes; the sprite sits on the paper, and the K1 band runs under both. Most room for text, loses the bay's count-up stage.
    Recommended: B. The number never moves, so planning (the ghost) and the count (the liquid) read as one gauge, it is the biggest of the three, and the bay keeps its juice.
    • Each beat of the count (0.95 s, unchanged) shows the item's name, its effect and only the step that just fired, in 16 px bold; the full list of steps stays for the scan (planning), where there is time to read it. The landing sticker ("+€10") slaps on the K1 column's label, never on the number, as in #86.
    • The step lines use the receipt's wording and order (base, each bonus, each multiplier with its giver, WASTED in tomato), so the scan, the count and the receipt read the same.
    • Not drawn yet: the coin fountain (WIN B) and the shine on landing (FONT C) from the monitor-juice decisions; they play on the K1 number as approved, in every layout.
