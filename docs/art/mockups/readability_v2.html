2Item tickets: the old price look, coloured by aisleQ1

You liked today's price: the base struck through with the new price in the gold burst. Every variant keeps exactly that, at today's sizes (22 px base, 60×32 burst with 19 px digits, overhanging the top-right corner). The colour answers your question "which colour when an item has several families?": the ticket takes the colour of the aisle the product is stocked in, which is always exactly one aisle (data/aisles); the glyphs then show every family it counts as. Staples have no aisle, so they have no colour: plain paper with the padlock among their glyphs. Where does the aisle colour go?

  • A · Header band. The top 13 art rows are the aisle colour and carry the struck base; the glyph column under the burst takes a pale tint of the same colour, so band and column make one L. The most colour, readable along the whole rail; the effect sits on paper beside the column.
  • B · Spine. Today's ticket, with the thin top band and a 4-art-px spine on the left in the aisle colour. The smallest change; the colour is a thin edge.
  • C · Price flag. A paper ticket whose struck base sits on an aisle-colour flag (a shelf-edge flag with a notched end). Colour only where the price is (and the pale glyph column).
Recommended: A. It is v1's header that you called "a good step", now with the price back at full size: the band holds the 22 px struck base and the burst overhangs it exactly as today. The colour is big enough to read the aisles along the rail at a glance; B's 8 px spine and C's flag are hard to tell apart at game size (Cold cases teal next to Household grey, Fruit & veg green next to Snacks brown).

The colour rule: one aisle colour per product, the glyphs carry every family

  • Why the aisle and not a family: no product is in two aisles (159 cards checked: 50 products × plain/BIG/GOLDEN, every tier in its base's aisle), so the colour never has to pick. A family colour would have to pick one tag and hide the others: Frozen peas would read "Frozen" while Lemon's "×2 next Produce" hits it.
  • The catch: every product whose aisle and families differ (all 44 aisle products checked in data/aisles and data/cards). The colour says where it lives, the glyph says what rules see; rules never test the aisle, so the glyph is the one to trust.
    • Crackers and Day-old buns: stocked in Pantry, count as Bakery.
    • Birthday candles: on the Bakery aisle, count as Household (not Food).
    • Ham ends: in Cold cases, but neither Dairy nor Frozen (Food, Clearance).
    • Tea bags: in Pantry, whose sign is the Food tin, but not Food (Breakfast only), so "×2 next Food" skips it.
    • Cold cases holds two themes: five Dairy products and Frozen peas, the only Frozen product (it is also Produce, so Lemon's "×2 next Produce" hits it).
    This is a note, not a question: if you would rather move a card to another aisle, that is a data change for later, with your approval.
  • No aisle: the six staples (Banana, Bread, Milk, Eggs, Coffee, Soup) have no band, spine or flag at all, just paper, and the padlock joins their glyphs, so "no colour" always means "no aisle" (their old cream band looked like Bakery's tan). A staple with three glyphs (Milk) puts the third left of the column on line 1. Coupons keep today's coupon ticket (teal dashes, COUPON).
  • Pantry, one shade darker: its sign colour is mustard, the same family as the gold burst, so its band uses the next shade down (mustard dark) and the burst stands clear (stress row, Day-old buns). Only Pantry: darkening every aisle one step would make Pantry and Bakery two near-identical browns.
  • The struck base: ink digits with a 1-texel paper outline on a colour band, so they read on grey, teal and mustard alike; the strike is tomato with an ink line under it, drawn low through the digits so a struck "€1" no longer reads as "€t".
  • Price sizes (worth deciding with Q1): today the new price (19 px digits in the burst) is smaller than the old one (22 px struck base), though it is the one you pay. Every variant keeps today's sizes as you asked; a later tweak could give the burst 22 px digits or the struck base 18 px, so the paid price is the biggest number. The wide burst (Golden Milk €126) also reaches over the struck "€6" with its left spikes; a 3-digit price would need the base set 4 px further left.
  • The family glyphs stack in a column on the ticket's right edge, under the burst (one glyph centred, two stacked), on a pale tint of the aisle colour instead of the old cream divider. Today's aisle glyph sat in the top-right corner and simply hid whenever the burst covered it; the column is never covered. Each inline glyph is glued to the word before it, so "beside " wraps as one piece and a glyph never sits alone on line 2.

4Bonus stickers and giver badges, polishedDEFAULT

v1's stickers were flat boxes with square corners and the giver mini on the top-right corner. Now they are built like the game's (ink outline, 0.42 ink shadow at (2, 2), the tilt list −4°, 3°, −3°, 4°, −2°) at v1's bigger size, with the giver badge back on the left, vertically centred, and polished so it reads at game size: corners rounded in a 3-step stair (2-1-1 texels), a full-white texel row along the top and left, a 2-texel lip at the bottom (cream, then darker) over a 1-texel inner ink line so the sticker has a thickness, a white specular tick on the burst, and an embossed number. Juice: a one-frame white flash as it lands, a pop-in that overshoots and settles (squash 1.2×0.8 → 0.92×1.08), 2 px impact dust under it, four gold sparks bursting out, a hard-edged pixel shine, a number punch, and the count's fire (the whole sticker punches and turns −6° with its text and badge, flash, green check). A giver's gold sparks (the game's GiverSparks) run from the giver to its sticker as it slaps on, and again in the count when the giver is scanned. WASTED gets none of the celebration: it drops in heavy, then a sad thud (a short darken, a 2 px shake) and a tomato underline.
