Next Customer: shift scene ambiance #123 · a mock-up of the lighting direction, on a real game capture

The shift scene is lit flat: the wall, the props and the work all sit at the same brightness, so nothing says where to look and nothing says when it is. This page takes the title's C+ storefront language (a light story, dither bands instead of gradients, a time of day) into the shift scene. It paints over the game's planning capture (docs/art/rule_tweaks_2/shift_screen_effects_1280x720.png), so every pixel of the UI is the game's. Only the light changes.

Hold Space or press on the stage to see the game as it is.

The rules every time of day keeps

  • The work is always lit. The monitor, the receipt and its printer, the belt and its tickets and stickers, the hand baskets, the keys and the top bar get no shade at all, at any time of day. Light only falls off around them, on the wall, the props and the floor. Show the clear zones draws them.
  • No light on the work either. Sun shafts and lamp pools stop at the clear zones too, so a highlight can never hide a ticket colour, a sticker or a receipt line.
  • One key light, upper left, as in the art bible. A high window is added on the back wall, between the receipt and the monitor. It is the only new prop, and it shows the sky.
  • Pixel art all the way through. Light works on the 640×360 art grid in four dither steps (25, 50 and 75 %) and snaps every colour to the palette, the way the title's night palette does. No soft gradients, no blur.
  • The props recede, the work comes forward. The cashier, the produce stand, the sign, the rewards machine and the cold cases take the shade, darkest at the edges and the ceiling.

Recommendation

Tie the time of day to the shift within the week: morning, midday, dusk, then night for the Manager's review. The week (#119) then reads in the scene itself, the review shift feels like the late, high-stakes one, and the night look comes back to the title's storefront. The clear zones make it safe: the scene changes, the work never does.

Build it as data, not as art per time of day. One shade field (base, edge falloff, ceiling) and a light list (window shafts, lamp pools, cold-case glow) per time of day in a .tres, applied by one shader on the scenery layer under the UI. The window's sky is its own small layer. The clear zones come from the scene's own rects (the monitor, the receipt column, the belt, the baskets, the column), as the crowd's avoid and cover do.

To decide

  1. Which time-of-day story? (A) The four shifts of a week run morning to night, as shown (recommended). (B) One fixed look all run (pick a tab). (C) It follows the run: day for week 1, dusk for week 2, night for week 3.
  2. How dark may the night get? As shown (the props almost gone, like the title), or a step lighter so the cashier and the stand still read.
  3. The high window: keep it (the only place the sky shows, so the time of day reads at a glance), or no window and only the light colour changes.
  4. Small life, later or now? A ceiling tube that flickers now and then at night, the sun shafts drifting slowly through a shift, dust motes in the shafts. Each is cheap, but each is motion on the screen while the player plans.
  5. The checkout crowd (#120): the standees stand in the shaded wall band between the receipt and the monitor, so in this look they would be drawn in the scene's shade (a darker ramp at dusk and night) and lit only when they cheer. Re-place their zone once the window is placed.
