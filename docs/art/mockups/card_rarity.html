Card rarity: the card itself carries its rarity

Your note on machine V3: "really good. Next I would love if cards embrace rarity instead of just having a halo." So the halo is gone, and the card itself changes with rarity: its paper, frame, art window, name band, tab and motion. A common card stays humble and clean, a rare card is clearly special, and a legendary card is a jackpot you can feel. Everything you approved stays in place: the 72×122 art px size and layout, the rarity tab and pips, the B2 family chips, the name in the kind-colour band, the rule, the A2 digits and the gold LAST ROW / BEST ROW sticker with its count-up.

Three directions were built and judged. CARD LOOK in the toolbar switches every picture on this page between them (or add ?look=A, B or C to the URL). C, the store's own promo language, is the recommendation and opens by default, with the judges' fixes and the best ideas from A and B added. Reduced motion shows the still version of each card.

Rule (decided 2026-11-03): a product's rarity is its tier. A plain product is always COMMON, its BIG tier is RARE and its GOLDEN tier (the golden sprite, e.g. milk_golden) is LEGENDARY. Coupons, upgrades and loyalty cards keep their own rarities. So a legendary product card always frames a GOLDEN sprite, and the card and the sprite have to work together.

C · Store promo language (recommended) Common is a shelf label on receipt paper with a barcode and a torn foot. Rare is a SPECIAL OFFER shelf talker with a hazard-stripe border, thin rays and a tilted RARE! price-gun flash. Legendary is the jackpot display: a dark engraved brass frame with the PREMIUM sign's running bulbs, diamond studs, a spotlit stage with glass and a pedestal, a LEGENDARY!! banner, a prize rosette and confetti.

1 · One product at each rarity, in colour and in grey

Milk at its three tiers: plain Milk (common), Big Milk (rare) and Golden Milk (legendary), with their names, prices and rules from data/cards/milk*.tres, then the same three cards in greyscale. The three rarities differ in outline and pattern, not only in colour: torn foot, striped border, bulb frame. Each card replays its landing every 5 s.

Gold on gold. A legendary product is always GOLDEN, so a gold sprite sits inside a gold card. The art window has to keep the two apart: Golden Milk's pale gable and light faces melted into the pale window, the gold rays and the foil. The fix, in all three looks: a GOLDEN sprite gets a 1 px ink outline, and the window keeps its ink and paper line inside the brass, so the sprite never touches the frame. Golden Milk on its legendary card in A, B and C, then the same three in grey. This strip ignores the CARD LOOK toggle.

2 · Every kind at its rarity

Crisps and the Breakfast sticker (common); Big Carrier bag, 2 for 1 and Mixed basket with its LAST ROW / BEST ROW sticker (rare); Shelf upgrade and Bakery aisle run with a WASTED row (legendary). The two mustard-kind legendaries were the hard case: C now keeps a 1 px ink and 1 px paper line between the brass and anything inside, so a mustard band or window never melts into the frame.

3 · In the machine

Both visits replay the drop every 3.6 s. On a Normal visit, the rare card is the only "talker" on the shelf. On a Boosted visit, the legendary card's bulbs light up from the top, the banner and rosette drop in, the spotlight comes on and the confetti flies. The confetti stays behind the cards and inside the bay.

5 · Recommendation: C, the store's own promo language

  • It is the only look where every rarity reads in a bay of 3 and in grey. A torn receipt, a striped talker and a bulb frame are three different outlines, so colour isn't needed.
  • It speaks the game's own language. A supermarket shows "special" with shelf talkers, price-gun flashes and lit displays, and the legendary bulbs step at the PREMIUM sign's 10 Hz, so the cards belong to the machine.
  • The halo's feeling is kept, inside the card. The old chase lights became the legendary frame's bulbs.
  • It frames the GOLDEN tier well. A legendary product is always GOLDEN. C's dark brass, the ink and paper line and the dim stage give the gold sprite the most contrast of the three looks, and the rarity's own gold stays in the frame, bulbs and banner.
  • Taken from A: the torn receipt foot on common, the dark engraved brass with diamond studs, the 1 px ink and paper line that keeps mustard kinds off the frame, the glass streaks in the spotlit window, the rarity-coloured pedestal strip, and the rule that a shine only lights brass and the window, never the rule.
  • Taken from B: every sweep is drawn under the art and the words, and the burst and confetti are drawn behind all three cards.
  • Judges' fixes in C: the rosette is smaller and sits on the bottom-left corner, clear of the chips, the SHELF +1 chip, the pedestal, the name and the sticker. SHELF +1 moves to the empty price corner. The rays are thinner (8, with the outer half dithered). The RARE! flash is a baked hard-pixel sprite at fixed angles, not a rotated canvas. The confetti is clipped to the bay. The upgrade pedestal is narrower. The dead halo code is removed.
  • Not taken: B's foil stripes over the band, its gold-fleck paper and the art breakout (it moves into the banner and needs per-sprite data). A's flecks behind the rule.

Sizes are art px (×2 for the 1280×720 stage). The card stays 72×122 with V3's positions. Only the frame grows outside the card: 0 px (common), 2 px (rare) and 3 px (legendary). The bay's tickets move 1 px inwards (x 324, 405, 486) so a legendary frame clears the bay's edge.

Open questions

  1. Rare stripes: they stay still at rest, crawl as the card lands and crawl while hovered. Or should they always crawl slowly?
  2. Legendary breakout: should the legendary product also climb 3–4 px over its window's top edge (from B)? It isn't in C for now, because it would run into the LEGENDARY!! banner.
  3. GOLDEN outline: the 1 px ink outline is only on the card, where the golden sprite meets a gold frame. Should the golden sprites carry it everywhere (cart, belt, receipt), or only on prize cards?
  4. Confetti: it is drawn behind the cards so it never covers a word. Do you want it in front of the cards for a bigger moment, at the cost of briefly covering the neighbours?
