THE IDEA

  • Today a sticker given by an item carries a cream token with the item's first two letters ("Eg"). Your idea: show a tiny drawing of the item instead, one egg for Eggs, one banana, the green tea box.
  • The row below is the real planning screen at its real size (one art pixel = 2 screen pixels). Switch the badge look with the buttons and watch the replay: the stickers slap on, then the pointer rests on four givers in turn (Golden Eggs, Tea bags, Mixed herbs, Cheese) and everything else greys out at once, as in the game.
  • The minis are drawn by hand at 7×7 art pixels (14 screen pixels), not shrunk from the sprites: at that size a shrunk 48 px sprite turns to mush. Alone (die-cut), a 7×7 mini with its 1 pixel paper ring is exactly today's 18 px badge. On the cream token it is not: the token needs a cream ring and its own rim, so the token grows to 22 px (11×11 art pixels).
  • Token (my pick) puts the mini on the cream round token, which grows from 18 to 22 px so the drawing has a cream ring round it; squeezed into today's 18 px token the mini's outline runs into the token's rim (see the sheet). Die-cut shows the mini alone with a paper ring, like a cut-out sticker, 18 px like today. I expected to prefer the die-cut, but over busy item art (the coffee bean on the Golden Eggs carton, the cheese on the coupon slip) it loses its edge; the cream disc keeps every mini readable.
  • A GOLDEN giver gets the same mini with a gold ring. Golden Eggs show "Go" today (its name starts with "Golden"), so the mini also fixes that.

DEFAULTS (SAY NO TO ANY)

  • One mini per product, shared by its BIG and GOLDEN copies. BIG looks like the plain one; only GOLDEN gets the gold ring.
  • Products with no sprite yet (the art-pending ones, like Mixed herbs here) keep their initials on today's token. When their sprite is drawn, their mini is drawn with it.
  • Coupon, loyalty and inspection badges stay as they are.
  • Greyed-out (another item focused), minis use today's grey, like the initials: they step back and are not meant to be read. Most keep a different shape (the upright egg, the tilted bean), but not all: greyed Tea bags and Milk are two grey boxes (see "Greyed, real size" in the sheet). A product with no sprite keeps its initials on the 22 px token too.
  • Coupon and loyalty badges stay 18 px, so with the token look a stack can mix 18 and 22 px badges (Milk's stack: the 22 px tea box over the 18 px stamp).
  • Each mini is a single 7×7 source in art/src/, placed on a new 11×11 token (and its GOLDEN copy with the gold ring). For the die-cut, the ring would be the game's existing one-pixel outline (the one round a selected basket item) in paper or gold.
  • The monitor's key line ("from an item") uses the same badge.

QUESTIONS

1. Which badge look?

★ Token: the mini on the cream token, 22 px. Cost: it is bigger than the 18 px coupon and loyalty badges, hangs 4 px further left, and a stack mixes the two sizes (see "Stacks 3x"). RECOMMENDED

Die-cut: the mini alone with a paper ring, 18 px like today, nothing in the stack moves.

Why: even with the redrawn Milk and Tea bags, a die-cut mini over a busy item (the coffee bean on the Golden Eggs carton, the cheese on the coupon slip) loses its edge; on the token every mini sits on the same calm cream disc.

2. Which products get a mini?

★ Every product that has a sprite (23 today), initials for the rest (about 34 products, until their sprite is drawn). RECOMMENDED

Only the products whose rules give stickers to other items (Eggs, Coffee, Tea bags, about 8).

Why: the Repeat coupon copies any product, and its "=€12" sticker shows the copied item's badge (Cheese in this row), so any product can end up on a badge.

3. Is the tier on the badge enough as a gold ring?

★ Yes: gold ring for GOLDEN, nothing for BIG. RECOMMENDED

Also mark BIG (a mustard ring), or tint the mini itself gold.

Why: the item and its ticket already wear the tier; tinting the mini gold makes the banana and the cheese hard to tell apart.

THE MINIS

Twelve hand-drawn minis in the game's palette, 7×7 art pixels with an ink outline and light from the top left, grouped by the look-alikes they must not be confused with. After review I redrew Milk (the carton with its tomato band, not a pale oval), Coffee (a tilted bean, so it can't pass for the upright egg), Tea bags (the green box with its tag: a single bag on a string read as a key or a music note at this size), Day-old buns (the film bag with its tomato tie), Bread (the crust dome) and Reduced yogurt (squatter). Each card shows the source at 8×, then on the dark belt the die-cut at its real size and, at 4×, the die-cut, the token, both GOLDEN versions and the greyed versions. The real sprite is beside it for reference.
